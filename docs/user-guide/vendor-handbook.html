The three screens

The whole portal is three tabs. Everything you do lives on the first one; the other two are there when you want to check a number.

TabWhat it holds
My jobs Your queue, plus two panels above it: Ready to send, which is the work waiting to be parcelled up, and Parcels, which is everything moving to or from your bench. Open any job for its items, artwork and photographs.
Rates Your rate card — what you are paid per piece, by stage and by size band. This is the figure a job is priced from at the moment we assign it to you.
Payments What is owed and what has been settled. A settled job is closed: it can no longer be edited, reassigned or put on a parcel.

Filter the queue by status and the address bar changes with it, so a filtered queue is a link you can send to somebody or keep in a tab.

The life of a job

A job moves through these in order. The colour tells you whose move it is at a glance: amber is yours. You will only ever see buttons for moves that are actually legal from where the job is standing — if a button is not there, that move is not available yet.

Assigned to you Your move

We have given you the work and priced it from your rate card. Nothing starts until you say you have what you need — the artwork for a print job, or the printed sheet for a frame job.

Press Mark received. It asks “Confirm you have everything you need to start?” Only say yes if you genuinely do.

In production Your move

The piece is on your bench. This is the only window in which you can add, replace or withdraw photographs — once the job leaves production the shot list is read-only.

When the work is finished and every required shot is uploaded, press Send for approval. The button stays disabled until the shot list is complete, and the screen names the shots still missing.

Sent for approval With us

We are looking at your photographs. Nothing is asked of you here. The job will move to Approved or Changes needed.

Changes needed Your move

We found something. The job screen leads with exactly what has to be put right — the defects we ticked, and any note the reviewer wrote.

Redo the work, photograph it again, and send it for approval once more. Every submission is kept: a rework that passes does not erase the failure that caused it, and the old photographs stay on file.

Approved — ready to ship Your move

The piece has passed. What happens next depends on whether you are the workshop assembling the order.

  • If the goods have to travel, the job appears in Ready to send. Send it as a parcel — see below.
  • If you are assembling the order, keep the piece. It waits on your bench for the rest to arrive, then goes out to the customer with a courier label we buy.
Handed over Finished

The piece has left your hands, either on a parcel to another workshop or with the courier. This is the end of the line — a job here cannot move again, and you are paid for it.

Cancelled Finished

The work was called off. If a price was agreed for work you had already done, that figure stands and is what you see on the job. Otherwise a cancelled job is not a bill.

Why raking light

A lamp held almost flat across the surface throws every scuff, dent and banding line into relief. Front-on light hides all three. It is the single shot that settles most disputes, on the print and on the glazing alike.

File rules

  • JPEG, PNG or WebP. Nothing else is accepted.
  • Not HEIC — which is what an iPhone shoots by default. Turn the camera to “Most Compatible”, or export as JPEG before uploading. A HEIC file is refused at the door rather than stored as something nobody can open.
  • Up to 25 MB per photograph. Do not downscale to fit — the detail is the point.
  • Up to 40 photographs per job, counting replaced ones. Reshooting the whole list repeatedly will use it up.

Uploading into a slot that already holds a shot replaces it. The earlier photograph is not deleted — it stays on file and we can still see it, which is why nothing is lost by correcting a bad shot.

Sending a parcel

When approved work has to travel to the workshop assembling the order, it appears under Ready to send on your jobs screen.

We group it, you do not

Each group is one parcel's worth of work, already worked out for you. You cannot pick jobs across groups and you do not need to — a group is exactly the set that belongs in one box. If a group holds two jobs, both pieces go in together.

What to fill in

FieldWhat we expect
Carrier Who is carrying it — Delhivery, Blue Dart, whoever you booked. Up to 120 characters. Leave it blank if you have not booked yet.
Docket reference The consignment or AWB number off the docket. Up to 120 characters. This is what we quote when we chase the carrier, so it is worth getting right.
Pieces How many physical boxes or tubes. It starts at the number of jobs in the group — change it if you packed them differently. Between 1 and 999.
Due by The date the carrier promised, off the docket. Not a promise about your own work.

Press Send this group. The jobs move to Handed over, the parcel appears in your Parcels panel as outbound, and you are paid for that work whatever happens to the box afterwards.

You do not choose where it goes

There is no destination field, and that is deliberate. We decide the routing and we pay for the leg, so there is nothing for you to select and no address for you to get wrong. Pack it, book it, record the docket.

Receiving a parcel

Anything on its way to you sits at the top of the Parcels panel, marked Coming to you, with the docket and the carrier's promised date.

When the box actually reaches your bench, press Confirm arrival. Do it on arrival, not when you get round to opening it — a frame job cannot start until the sheet it needs is confirmed as landed, and the sending workshop and we are both waiting on that one press.

  • “Not sent yet” means the parcel exists but nobody has handed it to a courier. There is nothing to confirm; it will gain a button once it is genuinely moving.
  • “Written off as lost” means the parcel is not coming. Do not wait for it and do not chase the courier — we raise a replacement job to cover the work, and nothing about the loss is charged to you.

What you are paid

Every job shows one figure under You are paid. That is the number, and it does not change because of anything that happens to the goods after they leave you.

  • Normally it is your rate card price, worked out from the stage and the biggest item's longest edge at the moment we assigned the job.
  • If a price was negotiated for that job, you see the agreed figure with the rate-card price shown beside it, so you can see both numbers in a disagreement.
  • A lost parcel does not cost you the job. The work was done; the box is our problem.
  • Unavailable means we could not read the figure, not that it is zero. Tell us if you see it.

When a button refuses

The portal tries to grey out anything that would be refused, but a job can move under you while your screen is open — someone here reassigns it, or a second tab gets there first. These are the messages you may see, and what each one actually means.

“These jobs have already been sent on a parcel, and a job rides exactly one.”

Someone already sent it — often you, in another tab. Refresh the page. If a parcel genuinely went missing, we raise a replacement job rather than a second parcel for the same work.

“Nobody has been chosen to assemble this order yet, so there is nowhere to send it.”

Wait — this one is ours. We have not yet decided which workshop assembles the order. Nothing is wrong with your work. It will appear in Ready to send once we have routed it.

“You are assembling this order yourself, so there is no parcel to send.”

Keep the pieces. You are the workshop the goods are travelling to. Hold them until the rest of the order arrives, then despatch the order itself.

“These jobs are not all on the same order, so they cannot ride one parcel.”

Send one parcel per group. You should not be able to reach this from the screen — if you do, refresh and send each group on its own.

“One of these jobs was cancelled, so there is nothing left to send.”

The work was called off after the screen loaded. Refresh, and check your queue for what is current.

“One of these jobs has already been settled and can no longer be moved.”

It is paid and closed. Do not re-open a settled job — raise it with us instead and we will sort it out properly.

“This parcel has not been sent yet, so there is nothing to confirm.”

It has not left the other bench. The confirm button appears once it is actually on its way.

“This parcel was already confirmed.”

Someone at your end already pressed it. Nothing more to do.

“Carrier is longer than 120 characters…”

Shorten that box. The message names which of the two fields is over — carrier or docket reference.

Anything else, or the same refusal twice after a refresh: send us the job number from the address bar and what you pressed. We can see both ends of every parcel and can tell you what is actually blocking it.

What you are not shown

Some things are missing from this portal on purpose, and it is worth knowing which, so you do not go looking for them.

  • No customer. No name, address, phone or order number reaches this portal. You will not find them by looking harder.
  • No other workshop. A parcel tells you the docket, the carrier, the piece count and whether it is coming to you or going from you — never who is at the other end. That protects you as much as them.
  • No retail prices. You see your rate, not what the piece sold for.
  • The courier label is an exception, and a narrow one. When you are the workshop shipping an order out, you can download the label — it carries the customer's address because the courier prints it. It arrives as a file to hand to the courier, is never displayed on screen, and each download is recorded.

If you need something chased with a carrier or another workshop, ask us. We can see both ends; you are not expected to.

Anything here that does not match what you see on screen is a mistake in this handbook, not in your reading of it — tell us and we will fix it. Keep this page bookmarked; it is updated as the portal changes.
